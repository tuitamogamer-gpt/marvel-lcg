# Magneto standalone original-source contract

This import covers the **26 English retail faces representing 30 physical cards**, `32138`–`32158`, in the Mutant Genesis **Magneto** scenario set. Three villain stages, three double-sided main schemes and two double-sided progress cards are staging pieces. The encounter deck receives **22 physical scenario cards**. Standard selects stages I/II and Expert selects II/III, so either game uses 29 of the 30 scenario cards. Acolytes is the recommended seven-card set in one replaceable modular slot; there is no mandatory second modular set. Future Past, campaign roles and Magneto's Fortress are outside this standalone contract.

Two corrections must remain separate from these immutable imports:

- **Published gameplay errata:** RRG 1.8 page 68 replaces the magnet-counter response on all three main-scheme B faces. The operative text is: “After you place a magnet counter on this scheme, if there are at least 3 magnet counters here, **remove 3 of them and discard cards from the encounter deck until a Magnetic card is discarded. Reveal that card.**” The old scan and raw JSON instead reveal the card before removing counters. The corrected playable text and rules handler follow the current erratum. The [official July 25, 2025 answer](https://hallofheroeslcg.com/latest-ffg-rulings-post-rrg-1-6/) also explicitly says to remove counters before resolving the Magnetic card. This prevents the old printing's unintended repeated response while the original three counters remain.
- **Printing transcription repair:** Electromagnetic Blast `32153` visibly prints “Place 1 **magnet** counter on the main scheme.” The downloaded JSON omitted “magnet.” Its presentation metadata restores the printed word; this is not gameplay errata.

Current RRG 1.8 also supersedes the old expansion insert's Teamwork definition. The [Acolytes contract](acolytes-integration.md) documents the current page 43 rule: only the entering minion activates when another matching-trait minion is in play. The old all-matching-minions wording must not be reinstated for this scenario.

## Setup, main-scheme advancement and counters

Asteroid M's setup sets the actual Orbital Decay physical card aside and reveals Boarding Party. Defeating Boarding Party flips that same card and reveals Sabotage Master Mold. Defeating Sabotage Master Mold reveals the set-aside Orbital Decay and adds the defeated Sabotage card to the victory display. Defeating Orbital Decay flips that same physical card and reveals Physical Strain attached to Magneto. These are successive faces and physical moves, not newly fabricated side schemes or attachments. Each side scheme is defeated normally; its When Defeated ability resolves before the card would leave play (RRG page 48).

On main-scheme advancement, RRG page 27 removes the old main card, returns **all its tokens except acceleration tokens** to the supply, and discards its attachments. Magnet counters do not carry to the next stage. Factory Online then places one new magnet counter; if Sabotage Master Mold is not in victory, the first player searches the actual encounter deck and discard for an M-Type Sentinel and reveals it. The Rule of Magnus places two new magnet counters; if Physical Strain is not attached to Magneto, the first player searches those zones for a Magnetic attachment and reveals it. The searched physical card must remain the same card, and the searched deck is shuffled when the search ability finishes, including an unsuccessful search. A failed search does not create a card or an unprinted Surge.

All magnet-counter placements use the current main scheme's corrected threshold response. Magnetic Mayhem's “place 1 … for each” uses the counted discarded Magnetic cards; unrelated cards merely containing “magnetic” in their title do not count. A counter-producing Magnetic card revealed by the threshold sees the counters remaining **after** the current erratum's removal. A revealed Electric Shock may advance the main scheme through threat; it does not carry the old scheme's counters forward or subtract three from a newly revealed scheme afterward.

Discard-until and fixed-number encounter discards stop at the first deck-exhaustion boundary under RRG page 17. Resolve the normal reset and acceleration token, but do not continue the old discard instruction through the recycled deck. A matching card discarded last remains the same found physical card even if that discard empties the deck. No match means no card to reveal; the three magnet counters were already removed under the erratum.

## Damage ceilings, Bubble and status cards

“Sustained damage” is maximum HP minus remaining HP (RRG page 42). The progress schemes cap that difference at 6N, 12N and 18N. It is a current character-state ceiling, not a per-attack allowance, cumulative lifetime damage, or extra armor HP. Stage advancement resets Magneto's damage for the new villain stage and does not carry excess damage across. Same-title villain stages retain attachments, statuses, counters and other non-damage tokens under RRG page 47; Physical Strain therefore continues removing Steady on the next Magneto stage.

The [official June 6, 2024 answer 205 and clarification 206](https://hallofheroeslcg.com/latest-ffg-rulings-post-rrg-1-5/#205) apply the damage ceiling **before Magnetic Bubble**. This supersedes the struck-through early answer 196 saying Bubble receives all dealt damage. In solo, Boarding Party plus five sustained damage means a six-damage hit can cause only one damage to be taken; Bubble receives one, not six. With the ceiling already reached, no positive taken-damage event remains for Bubble. This does not reduce the damage **dealt**: answer 206 and RRG page 14 distinguish a full dealt-damage receipt from the reduced or replaced damage taken. Piercing can therefore remove Tough on a positive-damage attack even when the sustained-damage ceiling prevents Magneto from taking that damage. The [official October 10, 2025 answer](https://hallofheroeslcg.com/latest-ffg-rulings-post-rrg-1-6/) additionally says that if Boarding Party was blanked while Magneto accumulated excess sustained damage, restoring its text immediately brings sustained damage back within the cap.

RRG page 14 orders damage-dealt interrupts, Tough, damage-taken interrupts, then damage placement. Bubble is a **would-take** replacement, so Tough prevents the damage first and leaves Bubble unchanged. Piercing removes Tough before an attack deals damage (page 32). Bubble redirects all damage that reaches its interrupt, from attacks or other damage sources, onto itself. If that placement brings its total to at least eight, discard Bubble; there is no eight-point absorption limit or overflow damage to Magneto. Its Retaliate grant ends when it leaves play.

Helmet prevents new Confused cards; Armor prevents new Stunned cards. They do **not** clear existing status cards. [Official answer 145, February 15, 2024](https://hallofheroeslcg.com/latest-ffg-rulings-post-rrg-1-5/#145), expressly confirms that a Steady Magneto retains an existing Confused card when Helmet attaches. Removing Steady is different: [the official March 16, 2025 answer](https://hallofheroeslcg.com/latest-ffg-rulings-post-rrg-1-6/) requires discarding down to the newly allowed status limit, normally one of each type. A retained single Stunned or Confused card becomes effective once Steady no longer applies.

Helmet and Armor each offer an optional **Hero Response after that player's hero makes a basic attack against Magneto**, costing one Energy, one Mental and one Physical resource. They are not general player-phase discard actions and cannot be removed for an ally attack, an attack event or an attack against a minion. Wild resources can satisfy the specified types; resource abilities retain their actual payment restrictions. Costs must be paid before discarding the same attachment. The official January 31 and March 6, 2025 answers explain that an earlier Forced Response which could not stun Magneto while Armor applied is not rechecked after the later optional paid response removes Armor.

Wrapped in Metal forbids the attached identity from thwarting, attacking, defending or recovering, including labeled abilities performed by the identity. Its own Action requires exhausting the identity and one Physical resource; it is usable in either form. This is not a prohibition against the player's allies using their own powers.

## Encounter, boost and defeating-player timing

Magneto's after-attack counter response requires a completed attack, regardless of whether it dealt damage. A Stun-replaced attack is not an attack. RRG page 16 explicitly routes “after [enemy] attacks you” to the final attacked player when cooperative defense changes the defender, including the controller of a defending ally. That player places the magnet counter and resolves any Magnetic card revealed by the threshold response; for example, Wrapped in Metal attaches to player 2 when their character defended an attack originally initiated against player 1. A different activation triggered during an activation waits until the current activation and all its triggered abilities finish (current RRG page 6); resolving a Magnetic card must not overwrite an unfinished outer boost/attack context.

- **M-Type Sentinel:** When Defeated gives Magneto Tough. Its star boost gives **Magneto**, specifically, Tough and an actual facedown boost card. This is an additional boost to the current Magneto activation when applicable. If a Villainous Acolyte resolves it, Magneto receives the card for his later activation; the Acolyte does not take that extra boost. RRG page 47 preserves villain references on minion boost cards.
- **Master of Magnetism:** move the actual topmost Magnetic card from encounter discard to Magneto as a facedown boost, then activate Magneto against the resolving player in their current form. The added card does not replace the ordinary boost. If no Magnetic discard exists, the activation still occurs.
- **Electric Shock:** in Alter-Ego, Confuse the identity and place one main-scheme threat per current magnet counter; in Hero form, Stun it and deal it one damage per current counter. The status does not depend on having counters. Ordinary prevention applies to the damage.
- **Electromagnetic Blast:** exhaust all upgrades and supports controlled by that player, then place one magnet counter. Its boost only exhausts the identity and has no numeric boost icons.
- **Metal Shards:** deal one damage to each character the resolving player controls, then place one magnet counter. Its boost adds a counter only if **that attack defeats an ally**. Separate boost damage, consequential damage, a minion defeat and a scheme activation do not satisfy that condition.
- **Magnetic Missile:** defeat an actual Sentinel minion in play, then take five damage, with the option to discard X actual hand cards to prevent X of that damage. RRG page 44's **Then** rule means no Sentinel defeated means no post-Then damage. The card still has Surge. The defeated minion's When Defeated resolves normally; M-Type Sentinel can give Magneto Tough. This scenario card must not be confused with the later Magneto **hero pack** card also named Magnetic Missile: that different card discards a minion and has separate errata on RRG page 69.
- **Magnetic Mayhem:** the actual defeating player discards the top four encounter cards, then places the counted magnet counters. Four is a fixed number, not per player. Its Amplify icon applies to ordinary boost resolution while active.
- **Magnetically Sealed:** add two threat per ally currently in play, across all players, to its fixed two starting threat. This is a reveal snapshot. Its boost exhausts only the resolving player's allies.
- **Seized!:** each player places the actual top six cards of their own deck facedown beneath the scheme. RRG page 17 lets the first player order unspecific “each player” resolution. Defeating it activates Magneto against the **actual defeating player**. The scheme and stored cards remain until this When Defeated ability finishes, including the activation; then discard the stored physical cards to their owners' discard piles and discard the scheme. RRG pages 6 and 48 place the end of the activation before the defeated card leaves play. Faces beneath it remain hidden. For a deck with fewer than six cards, the implementation captures the available top-card cohort and performs the native empty-deck reset and encounter deal; this follows the bounded “top six” instruction and “resolve as fully as possible,” rather than applying the separate discard-exhaustion rule to a non-discard effect. No Seized-specific official exhaustion ruling was found in the retained rulings.
