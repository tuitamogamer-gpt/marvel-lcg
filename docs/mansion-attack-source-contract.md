# Mansion Attack standalone original-source contract

This import covers **26 English retail faces representing 25 physical cards**, `32121a/b`–`32129a/b` and `32130`–`32137`. The four double-sided villains, five double-sided main schemes and Save the School are staging pieces; the encounter deck receives the other **15 physical scenario cards**. Brotherhood is a required eight-card set. Mystique is the recommended five-card set in one replaceable modular slot. The Mutant Genesis campaign, Surprise Attack, role cards, Future Past and the Magneto villain scenario are outside this standalone contract.

## Setup, hidden order and terminal conditions

Main `1A` puts Save the School into play, shuffles the four location `2A` cards and stacks them beneath the opening scheme, then shuffles all four villains **without looking**. Use A sides in Standard and B sides in Expert; only the top villain is in play. The three other villains and unrevealed locations remain hidden. The four identical location fronts must not expose their reverse room names through card labels or an ordered upcoming-room list. Native seeded randomness may reproduce a save; it must not turn the order into public information.

Main `1B` deals each player one facedown encounter card, advances to the next main-scheme card, then adds itself to the victory display. Each `2A` flips to its `2B` room. A room starts with N threat, receives N ordinary escalation, and completes at 7N. Each completed room enters the victory display and advances. The printed loss condition counts **all main schemes in victory**, including the opening `1B`: three total therefore means **two completed rooms**, as the rulebook explicitly confirms. Acceleration tokens remain through main-scheme advancement under RRG page 5.

Save the School's Forced Response checks the number of **villain cards**, not victory points or total victory-display cards. Rulebook page 15 defines X: **Skirmish 1, Standard 2, Expert 3, Heroic 4**. The native Standard/Expert implementation uses 2/3. A defeated villain's Victory 2 puts its actual physical card in the victory display before this after-defeat response checks the total. At X, the players win without revealing another villain or dealing another round of encounter cards. Otherwise, deal each player an encounter card and reveal the next physical villain from the hidden deck.

The new villain is a **different character**. RRG “Villain Defeat,” page 47, says damage, ordinary attachments, upgrades, statuses, counters and non-damage tokens do not transfer to a different-title villain. A villain defeated while activating ends that activation without resolving it; the successor does not inherit the unfinished activation or its boost cards. The new villain enters with its own HP and Toughness. [Official answer 165, May 10, 2024](https://hallofheroeslcg.com/latest-ffg-rulings-post-rrg-1-5/#165), expressly covering Mansion Attack, preserves a Permanent attachment in play and attaches it to the next eligible villain. This exception does not transfer ordinary attachments.

If an engaged minion shares the new villain's title, Save the School discards that same minion and makes the **new villain activate against that minion's player**, using their form to determine attack or scheme. This is not a minion defeat. A minion controlled as an ally is not engaged with a player: [official answer 34, June 27, 2023](https://hallofheroeslcg.com/latest-ffg-rulings-post-rrg-1-5/#34), explicitly permits Karma's controlled Blob ally to coexist with the Blob villain.

The converse case uses RRG “Unique Icon,” page 46. Revealing a unique minion whose matching villain is already in play discards that minion, ignores its entry/reveal effects, and deals the revealing player a facedown encounter card. It does **not** create a villain activation. An attempted put-into-play that uniqueness forbids does not gain that reveal-only replacement deal.

## Room grants and attack timing

The Atrium, Cafeteria and Courtyard affect all current characters: identities, allies, minions and the active villain. The Basketball Court grants Toughness only to allies and minions. These are active constant grants, ending when the granting room leaves play or its ability text is blanked. Blanking a recipient's own printed text does not remove an external room grant. Retaliate values add to existing Retaliate, rather than causing separate damage instances; +1 ATK affects basic attacks and enemy ATK calculations, not unrelated fixed damage abilities.

RRG “Toughness,” page 45, defines a Forced Response **after the character enters play**. Gaining Toughness after entry, revealing Basketball Court with existing allies/minions, or restoring blanked room text does not retroactively give Tough. A later entering ally/minion gains Tough through the active room, subject to normal status limits. Removing or blanking Toughness does not remove an already acquired Tough status.

Steady permits a second Stunned and Confused card, requires two for the status to apply, and removes all of that type when the status cancels an action. [The exact official March 16, 2025 answer](https://hallofheroeslcg.com/latest-ffg-rulings-post-rrg-1-6/) says that when a character loses the ability allowing multiple statuses, it **must discard down to the allowed limit, usually one**. Therefore a location change or blanking the Atrium removes that permission immediately, while any separate active Steady grant still applies. A single remaining Stunned/Confused card becomes effective once no Steady grant remains.

RRG page 48 says alternate win and loss conditions cannot be blanked or canceled. This protects the actual X-villain win and three-main-scheme loss conditions; it does not provide a general rule that all of Save the School's text or all room advancement text is unblankable. The normal printed Victory keyword is ability text. A hypothetical blanking interaction must not silently turn ordinary Victory into an unblankable printed icon or broaden the alternative-condition rule to unrelated effects.

The four villain responses require an actual attack, not a Stun replacement or a separate scheme. RRG “Defend,” page 15, explicitly says that **after an enemy attacks you**, “you” is the final attacked player after another player's character or defense ability makes them the defender. Defending with that player's own ally still counts as attacking that player. Do not route these responses to the original activation player after cooperative defense changes the target.

- **Avalanche:** after attacking that player, exhaust an ally they control. No attack damage is required. This differs from the Brotherhood minion, which can exhaust any controlled character.
- **Blob:** after the attack damages a character, stun that actual character. Tough, complete prevention and zero damage do not qualify. Actual Overkill damage can qualify; separate boost damage cannot, following RRG page 9's identical Sonic Converter example.
- **Pyro:** after attacking that player, discard the actual top two cards of their deck and take indirect damage equal to the sum of their **printed resource icons**. Double-resource cards contribute two; boost icons and generated-resource multipliers do not count. Discarding stops when the current player deck empties; perform the native reset and extra encounter deal, but do not continue discarding from the recycled deck (RRG page 33). An infiltrating Shapeshifter discarded from the player deck returns to encounter discard under rulebook page 7 and contributes zero printed resources.
- **Toad:** after the attack damages a character controlled by that attacked player, discard one random card from their hand. Preserve control at the damage window even if the damaged ally is defeated. Empty hands do not create a card or a discard. Multiple damaged characters from one attack do not turn this single response into repeated random discards.

Indirect damage is assigned by the affected player among characters they control, up to each character's remaining HP, then resolved simultaneously. Tough can receive an assignment up to remaining HP and prevent that assigned damage; it does not permit unlimited assignment. Damage from Pyro's after-attack response is separate from the attack and cannot retrospectively satisfy another attacks-and-damages trigger.

RRG pages 9 and 40 put simultaneous forced after-attack abilities in first-player-chosen order. [Official answer 47, July 13, 2023](https://hallofheroeslcg.com/latest-ffg-rulings-post-rrg-1-5/#47), confirms that if Retaliate defeats Ultron I before his after-attack response initiates, no Drone enters. The same source principle applies to Cafeteria: resolving Retaliate first can remove a villain before its still-uninitiated printed response. A completed-attack receipt alone must not be treated as automatic prior initiation of every eligible response.

## Treacheries, boosts and side schemes

Brotherhood Beatdown resolves each listed effect for each named enemy in play: Avalanche exhausts the resolving player's identity; Blob stuns that identity; Pyro deals that player two indirect damage; Toad discards one random card from their hand. The named character can be the villain or a Brotherhood minion engaged with **any** player. The [exact official October 10, 2025 answer](https://hallofheroeslcg.com/latest-ffg-rulings-post-rrg-1-6/) explicitly confirms that Avalanche villain plus Pyro minion means exhaust the identity **and** take two indirect damage. Current RRG 1.8 page 36 now refers an encounter card's named references to encounter cards; no scenario-set-only restriction may be imposed.

Ground Swell, Immovable, Pyromaniac and Hopping Mad activate the named enemy against the **resolving player**, even if that minion is engaged with a different player. The current form determines attack or scheme; native Stun/Confuse replacements apply. If that named enemy is not in play, search the actual encounter deck **and** discard for its minion and **reveal the same physical card**. A reveal resolves native entry keywords and any active The Brotherhood Quickstrike grant. The search itself does not grant an extra activation to the newly revealed minion. A failed search creates no card and has no invented Surge. RRG “Search” and “Shuffle,” pages 39–40, shuffle the searched deck after the search ability finishes, including a failed search.

Each named treachery's boost has one numeric icon and a star. Only when the attacking/scheming **villain has that matching title** does the star give an additional boost card for that activation. Resolve that newly dealt boost in ordinary dealt order; the original numeric icon still counts. A minion's coincidentally matching name is not the villain condition. Ordinary When Revealed activation/search text is ignored when the card is a boost. Brotherhood Beatdown's boost is two ordinary icons with no star.

Protect the Students starts with **2 + 2N** threat and Crisis. Its When Defeated belongs to the actual defeating player, including their ally or another card they control. That player searches their own actual deck and discard for an ally, adds the chosen physical card to hand, then shuffles their searched deck. It does not put the ally into play or use the first player by default.

Under Siege has zero base threat and adds **3 threat for each Brotherhood of Mutants character currently in play** when revealed. This includes the active villain, applicable minions such as Mystique, and any other actual character with that trait. Hidden villains, defeated villains in victory and other out-of-play cards do not count. This is a snapshot reveal effect, not an ongoing threat adjustment when characters enter or leave. Its printed Acceleration icon remains separate from its text and contributes ordinary main-scheme escalation.
