# Bishop native integration

Bishop's Age of Apocalypse hero/player slice is `45001a/b` through `45029`: **30 faces on 47 physical cards**, including one double-sided identity, the unchanged forty-card Leadership source list, one obligation and five nemesis cards. The six existing-equivalent faces (`45013`, `45018`, `45019`, `45022`–`45024`) retain their original printing IDs; `bishop.ts` authors the other 24 faces. This implements neither the Age of Apocalypse scenarios nor its campaign.

Energy Absorption resolves after actual attack damage and before the attack's completion/Retaliate. Its discard batch stops at the initial deck boundary, allows the immediate deck reset and facedown encounter, then retrieves each actual discarded resource card from its current deck/discard location. Resource-card type is distinct from printed icon count. The optional Uniform counts resource cards in hand after retrieval. Lucas's form response returns an actual Temporal discard. Source IDs survive decisions and JSON reloads.

Both Super-Charged copies can modify one basic attack, each capped at +8 ATK. Malcolm and Randall discard resource cards as costs, use printed matching icons for healing and retain separate once-per-phase limits. The Rifle counts resource cards, gains Ranged and can initiate a zero-damage attack to remove Stunned. Concussive Blast and Command Authority retain a receipt for actual resource-card payment; a borrowed Concussive Blast readies the associated Bishop hero rather than the player resolving the event.

An entirely overpaid resource card does not grant either event's bonus. When the selected sources permit both outcomes, a saved allocation choice records whether a resource card contributes an icon to the actual cost, even when its icons match other sources. Every selected card is still spent once; the receipt preserves the physical source IDs and existing payment choices.

Energy Conversion is a native enemy-attack interrupt and event defense. It shuffles actual resource cards from discard into the deck and limits damage taken before Tough and prevention windows, preserving the original damage-dealt calculation. Its 3-damage allowance belongs to that actual attack ID. Side Step can prevent the entire capped amount; a later attack receives no residual allowance.

Advanced Suit uses current X-Men/X-Force traits, including trait-granting upgrades. Sidekick can attach to any identity-specific ally its player controls, including a teammate's signature ally, and supplies its actual +2 HP and recovery response. Side-by-Side commits its sidekick-ready additional cost with payment, before card-play responses; cancellation spends nothing and free play still pays that extra cost. It then offers healing or a phase-bound bonus to both actual characters. The ally's bonus persists through control changes and ends when that ally leaves play, including before the same physical card enters again. Ally attack/thwart responses resolve before consequential damage under Rules Reference 1.8 p13.

Side-by-Side checks actual ready eligibility both before initiation and when committing payment. The Merc with the Mouth and Restrained prevent that cost; a failed commitment leaves the event and resource cards in hand. The same ready guard suppresses X-23's optional ready response when she cannot ready.

Suit Up applies FFG's October 10, 2025 clarification/planned erratum: its searched upgrade must have an instruction allowing attachment to **an ally**; it need not match the ally found by that search. The runtime overlay cites this ruling while leaving imported JSON intact. Searching only an ally remains possible. Actual subsequent attachment still enforces eligibility, ownership and limits.

The obligation offers its optional alter-ego change before the two printed choices. A failed resource discard adds Surge. Nemesis setup uses five distinct owned pieces, stages pending reveals in the resolving zone, and moves those same IDs through Shadows of the Past. Portal Through Time grants Surge once per phase across all players. Trevor Fitzroy's ally-defeat receipt waits for his completed attack before resolving its forced response. Bantam finds the actual Portal; Temporal Trickery requires a maximum-icon hand card and places that many threat on every scheme.

Rules evidence: [Rules Reference 1.8](https://hallofheroeslcg.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/mc_rulesreference_v18_compressed.pdf), especially p13 consequential damage, p23 identity faces, p32 player-deck exhaustion and p36 referential abilities; [FFG post-1.5 rulings](https://hallofheroeslcg.com/latest-ffg-rulings-post-rrg-1-5/), May 10/June 6, 2024, for Bishop, Super-Charged, Rifle and Energy Conversion; [FFG post-1.6 rulings](https://hallofheroeslcg.com/latest-ffg-rulings-post-rrg-1-6/) for Suit Up. The named-Bishop behavior is an application of the reference rules, not a separate published Concussive Blast ruling.

The focused native suite covers source registration, all five aspect defaults, actual costs and targets, independent physical IDs, saved decisions, empty-deck retrieval, prevention order, borrowed-card identity references, player side schemes and nemesis timing. Full batch regression, source-mission coverage, browser acceptance and production verification remain separate release gates.

The isolated worker checkpoint passes TypeScript and **222 targeted tests across five files**, comprising sixty Bishop cases and 162 existing Cable, X-23, Vision and GMW host regressions. The original-source seed `45009` naturally reaches attack damage and returns a resource card through Energy Absorption after a public basic attack and end-turn sequence. This evidence applies to the worker integration checkpoint, not to later merged batch or production certification.

The focused follow-up passes **65 Bishop cases** and TypeScript, adding ready-restriction and actual-resource-card allocation regressions without rerunning the batch-wide gates.
